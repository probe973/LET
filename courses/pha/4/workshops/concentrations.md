## Calculating Dosage

The following formula is useful:

$$\text{Dose} = \frac{\text{What is needed}}{\text{What you have}} \times {\text{What it's supplied in}}$$

The **need** and **have** must be in the same unit, and the dose will be in the unit of **supplied in**.


{% include question_numerical.html
    id="pha4w03q6"
    title="6"
    question_text="A patient requires a drug dose of 200 mg.  The drug comes in liquid form with a strength of 5 mg/ml.  How many ml does the patient need? "
    correct_answer="40"
    tolerance="0"
    solution_text="$$ \frac{200 \text{ mg}}{5 \text{ mg}} \times 1 \text{ ml} = 40 \text{ ml}$$"
%}

{% include question_numerical.html
    id="pha4w03q7"
    title="7"
    question_text="A patient requires a drug dose of 500 mg.  The drug comes in liquid form with a strength of 2 % w/v.  How many ml does the patient need? "
    correct_answer="25"
    tolerance="0"
    solution_text="2 % w/v means 2 g in 100 ml

$$ \frac{500 \text{ mg}}{2 \text{ g}} \times 100 \text{ ml} = \frac{500 \text{ mg}}{2000 \text{ mg}} \times 100 \text{ ml} = 25 \text{ ml}$$"
%}

{% include question_numerical.html
    id="pha4w03q8"
    title="8"
    question_text="A patient requires a drug dose of 2 g.  The drug comes in liquid form with a strength of 800 mg/10 ml.  How many ml does the patient need? "
    correct_answer="25"
    tolerance="0"
    solution_text="
$$ \frac{2 \text{ g}}{800 \text{ mg}} \times 10 \text{ ml} = \frac{2000 \text{ mg}}{800 \text{ mg}} \times 10 \text{ ml} = 25 \text{ ml}$$"
%}

{% include question_numerical.html
    id="pha4w03q9"
    title="9"
    question_text="A patient is given a drug that comes in a strength of 0.4% w/v.  The patient needs 20 mg of the drug, twice daily for 7 days.  What is the total number of ml required for the treatment?"
    correct_answer="70"
    tolerance="0"
    solution_text="Need 20 mg twice daily for 7 days:

$$\text{Need} = 20 \times 2 \times 7 = 280 \text{ mg}$$

0.4 % w/v means 0.4 g in 100 ml

$$ \frac{280 \text{ mg}}{0.4 \text{ g}} \times 100 \text{ ml} = \frac{280 \text{ mg}}{400 \text{ mg}} \times 100 \text{ ml} = 70 \text{ ml}$$"
%}

{% include question_numerical.html
    id="pha4w03q10"
    title="10"
    question_text="A particular drug comes in a strength of 10 % v/v.  The patient needs an active dose of 5 ml per day for 5 days, followed by 10 days of 2.5 ml per day.  What is the total ml needed to complete the treatment? "
    correct_answer="70"
    tolerance="0"
    solution_text="Need 5 ml daily for 5 days then 2.5 ml daily for 10 days:

$$\text{Need} = 5 \times 5 + 2.5 \times 10 = 50 \text{ ml}$$

10 % v/v means 10 ml in 100 ml

$$ \frac{50 \text{ ml}}{10 \text{ ml}} \times 100 \text{ ml} = 500 \text{ ml}$$"
%}
