Brackets can be thought of as *mathematical puncuation* and helps us read an expression. It is sometimes useful to remove these brackets, and in mathematics we call this **expanding the brackets**. ## A single pair of brackets Consider the expression below: $$5(3x+4)$$ You can read this as 5 lots of the $3x+4$ bit. In other words, you will have 5 lots of the $3x$ and 5 lots of the $4$. This is knows as **distributing** the 5 through the bracketed expression: $$5(3x+4) \equiv 5 \times 3x + 5 \times 4 \equiv 15x + 20$$ Note that you are using a $\equiv$ sign rather than an $=$ sign because the statements above are exactly the same for all values of $x$, you are not trying to *solve* for a particular value of $x$. However, using an $=$ in your work would very unlikely be penalised. ### Examples **Expand $3x(5y-2x)$** Distribute the $3x$ throughout the $5y-2x$: $$3x(5y-2x) \equiv 3x \times 5y + 3x \times (-2x) \equiv 15xy - 6x^2$$ **Expand and simplify $x(5x-2)+6x$** Note that the $6x$ part is not part of the bracket, therefore the $x$ is **not** distributed to this part: $$x(5x-2) + 6x \equiv 5x^2-2x+6x \equiv 5x^2+4x$$ **Expand and simplify $4xy - 3y(5t-2x)$** $$4xy - 3y(5t-2x) \equiv 4xy + (-3y) \times 5t + (-3y) \times (-2x) \equiv 4xy - 9ty + 6xy \equiv 10xy - 9ty$$ --- ## Adding and subtracting single pairs of brackets The ideas above can be extended to more than one pair of brackets in a *mathematical sentence*. Consider: $$8(3x+4) + 6(2x-7)$$ Here you would distribute the 8 through the $3x+4$ and then distribute the 6 through the $2x-7$. You can then simplify if needed: $$8(3x+4) + 6(2x-7) \equiv 24x+32+12x-42 \equiv 36x-10$$ ### Example **Expand and simplify $x(2x-4)-(y-3x)$** $$x(2x-4)-(y-3x) \equiv 2x^2-4x - y + 3x \equiv 2x^2-x-y$$ Note that the second bracket pair is being multiplied by negative 1 here. --- ## Multiplying pairs of brackets Consider the following expression: $$(2x+5)(x-4)$$ Here you have one pair of brackets, $2x+5$, **all** being multiplied by another pair of brackets, $x-4$. Everything in the first bracket needs to be multiplied by everything in the second bracket. You can therefore think of this as distributing the $2x$ throughout the $x-4$ bracket and then distributing the $5$ throughout the $x-4$ bracket: $$(2x+5)(x-4) \equiv 2x(x-4) + 5(x-4)$$ This can now be completed in the same way as the examples in the previous section above. Alternatively, some use the acronym **FOIL**, which stands for: **F**irst **O**utside **I**nside **L**ast Here: **F**: $2x \times x \equiv 2x^2$ **O**: $2x \times (-4) \equiv -8x$ **I**: $5 \times x \equiv 5x$ **L**: $5 \times (-4) \equiv -20$ Putting this together you get: $$(2x+5)(x-4) \equiv 2x^2 - 8x + 5x - 20 \equiv 2x^2 - 3x - 20$$
