# Measures of Central Tendency
## Introduction When analysing data, it is often useful to identify a value that represents the centre of a dataset. Measures of central tendency provide a way of describing a typical or central value, allowing large datasets to be summarised using a single figure. The three most common measures of central tendency are: - Mean - Median - Mode Each measure describes the centre of a dataset in a different way. Choosing the most appropriate measure depends on the type of data and the distribution of the values. --- ## What Is Central Tendency? Central tendency refers to the tendency of data values to cluster around a central or typical value. Consider the following dataset representing the number of books borrowed by five learners: $$ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 4,\ 7 $$ Rather than describing every value individually, a measure of central tendency can be used to provide a summary of the dataset. A measure of central tendency should help answer the question: > What value best represents the centre of this dataset? --- ## The Mean ### What Is the Mean? The mean is commonly referred to as the **average**. It is calculated by adding all values together and dividing by the number of values. ### Formula $$ \text{Mean} = \frac{\sum x}{n} $$ Where: - $\sum x$ is the sum of all values. - $n$ is the number of values. ### Example Calculation Dataset: $$ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10,\ 12 $$ Step 1: Add the values. $$ 4 + 6 + 8 + 10 + 12 = 40 $$ Step 2: Count the number of values. $$ n = 5 $$ Step 3: Divide the total by the number of values. $$ \text{Mean} = \frac{40}{5} = 8 $$ ### Interpretation The average value in the dataset is 8. ### Strengths of the Mean - Uses every value in the dataset. - Reflects changes in all observations. - Provides a useful summary when data are evenly distributed and free from extreme values. - Forms the basis of many statistical techniques. ### Limitations of the Mean The mean is affected by extreme values (outliers). Consider the following dataset: $$ 8,\ 9,\ 9,\ 10,\ 54 $$ The mean is: $$ \frac{8+9+9+10+54}{5} = \frac{90}{5} = 18 $$ Although the mean is 18, most values are close to 9 or 10. The unusually large value of 54 pulls the mean upwards and makes it less representative of the typical value. ### When Should the Mean Be Used? The mean is most appropriate when: - Data are quantitative. - Data are interval or ratio level. - Extreme values are absent or limited. - The distribution is reasonably symmetrical. Examples include: - Assessment scores. - Heights. - Weights. - Completion times. --- ## The Median ### What Is the Median? The median is the middle value when data are arranged in order. Unlike the mean, the median is not heavily influenced by extreme values. ### Finding the Median Step 1: Arrange the data in order. Step 2: Locate the middle value. ### Example Calculation Dataset: $$ 3,\ 5,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10 $$ The values are already in order. The middle value is: $$ 6 $$ Therefore: $$ \text{Median} = 6 $$ ### Example with an Even Number of Values Dataset: $$ 2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8 $$ There are two middle values: $$ 4 \text{ and } 6 $$ The median is the mean of these two values: $$ \text{Median} = \frac{4+6}{2} = 5 $$ ### Why the Median Is Useful Consider the dataset: $$ 8,\ 9,\ 9,\ 10,\ 54 $$ The median is: $$ 9 $$ This is much closer to where most of the data are located than the mean of 18. ### Strengths of the Median - It is resistant to extreme values (outliers). - It often provides a more representative measure of centre when data are skewed. - It can be used with ordinal as well as quantitative data. ### Limitations of the Median - The median is based on the middle position of the data rather than every individual value. - Datasets with very different distributions can sometimes have the same median. - Changes to values away from the centre of the dataset may have little or no effect on the median. ### When Should the Median Be Used? The median is most appropriate when: - The dataset contains outliers. - The data are skewed. - The variable is ordinal. Examples include: - Income data. - Property prices. - Satisfaction ratings. - Confidence scales. --- ## The Mode ### What Is the Mode? The mode is the value that occurs most frequently in a dataset. Unlike the mean and median, the mode can be used with both numerical and categorical data. ### Example Calculation Dataset: $$ 2,\ 3,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5 $$ The value occurring most often is: $$ 3 $$ Therefore: $$ \text{Mode} = 3 $$ ### Example with Qualitative Data A survey records the preferred device type of learners. Responses: - Laptop - Tablet - Laptop - Desktop - Laptop The most common response is: $$ \text{Laptop} $$ Therefore, the mode is Laptop. ### More Than One Mode Some datasets contain multiple modes. Dataset: $$ 2,\ 2,\ 4,\ 4,\ 6,\ 7 $$ The values 2 and 4 both occur twice. The dataset is **bimodal** because it has two modes. ### Strengths of the Mode - Easy to identify. - Can be used with qualitative data. - Represents the most common observation. ### Limitations of the Mode - Some datasets have no mode. - Some datasets have multiple modes. - Does not consider all values. ### When Should the Mode Be Used? The mode is most appropriate when: - Identifying the most common category. - Analysing nominal data. - Determining the most frequent outcome. Examples include: - Most common transport method. - Most common eye colour. - Most commonly used device type. --- ## Comparing the Mean, Median and Mode
Measure Description Best Used For Affected by Outliers?
Mean The arithmetic average. Interval and ratio data with few extreme values. Yes
Median The middle value in an ordered dataset. Skewed data or datasets containing outliers. No, or only slightly
Mode The most frequently occurring value. Nominal, ordinal, quantitative or qualitative data. No
--- ## Choosing the Appropriate Measure
Situation Recommended Measure Reason
Assessment scores with no extreme values Mean Uses all data values and provides a precise average.
Income data with very high earners present Median Not distorted by extreme values.
Satisfaction ratings (Low, Medium, High) Median Suitable for ordered categories.
Most commonly used device type Mode Identifies the most frequent category.
Most common assessment score Mode Identifies the most frequently occurring value.
--- ## Summary Measures of central tendency provide a way of describing the centre or typical value of a dataset. - The **mean** uses every value and is often referred to as the average. - The **median** identifies the middle value and is resistant to extreme values. - The **mode** identifies the most frequently occurring value and can be used with categorical data. Choosing the most appropriate measure depends on both the type of data and the distribution of the values. A good analyst understands the strengths and limitations of each measure and selects the one that provides the most meaningful summary of the dataset.
